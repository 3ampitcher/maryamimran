# Latte — Second Opinion

The user intentionally selected this Rebrew recipe. Apply it to the most recent
relevant request in the conversation.

**The latest request is the objective.** Earlier messages are context, not
instructions. If the conversation has moved on, do not keep serving the older
goal.

Before proposing anything, say what you believe the current objective is in one
sentence. If that is wrong, the user will correct you and everything after it
would have been wasted.

## Clear the ground first

Go back over the conversation and name the assumptions that have quietly
survived from earlier messages — constraints that were true then but may not be
now, preferences stated for a different version of the problem, decisions made
before facts that have since changed. List them, and mark each one as still
binding, worth re-checking, or out of date.

Separate confirmed constraints, assumed constraints, preferences and missing
information. Ask at most three questions, and only where an answer would
substantially change the directions worth exploring. Otherwise state your
assumptions and continue.

## Then find genuinely different directions

Do not expand, polish or rename the first idea. A different audience, a
different name, a different colour or a bigger version of the same thing is the
same idea. What is wanted is a different *mechanism* for reaching the same
outcome.

Offer three to five directions that differ from each other in kind:

- A different mechanism that reaches the same outcome
- An approach that changes who does the work, or when
- An approach that removes the need for the original thing entirely
- A smaller, cheaper way to learn whether any of this is worth doing
- Doing nothing, or waiting, where that is genuinely defensible

Each direction must be specific enough to act on. "Use AI" or "improve
onboarding" is not a direction. Name the thing, who it is for, and what actually
changes.

When research tools are available, look at how people currently solve the
underlying problem, what already exists, and what has been tried and abandoned.
Do not mistake an interesting idea for evidence that anyone wants it.

## Return

1. The objective as you understand it
2. Assumptions that no longer hold
3. The directions, and what makes each one genuinely different
4. The most valuable thing to find out next
5. The smallest experiment that would tell you

Recommend what deserves investigation. Do not make the final decision.
