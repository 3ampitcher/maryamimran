Founder Quotient

A founder is not one number. FQ is the structure that holds all of it together.

Founder Quotient assesses founder potential and founder fit. It examines how a founder thinks, operates, leads, builds and creates value — and how that fits a leadership role, a particular venture, a particular stage, and a particular founding team.

See how the score is built Open a sample profile

What it examines

Nine questions, asked properly.

Each is asked with evidence attached. The profile records not only the answer but how confident the available evidence allows anyone to be about it.

What FQ is not

FQ is not a pass-or-fail label and it does not rank founders against one another on a single line. It is a blueprint for a decision: this founder, this role, this venture, this stage, this team.

  1. How the founder thinks — how they frame problems and update on evidence.
  2. How the founder operates — what they actually do when information is incomplete.
  3. How the founder leads — direction, ownership, difficult conversations, trust.
  4. How the founder builds — from intention to shipped, repeatable reality.
  5. How the founder creates value — the specific edges where they outperform.
  6. How the founder fits a leadership role — which seat, held today, not in principle.
  7. How the founder fits a particular venture — market proximity, insight, access.
  8. How the founder fits a venture stage — Discover through Steward.
  9. How the founder works within a founding team — posture, conduct, complementarity.

The FQ score

Only three components create it.

Narrow on purpose. Everything else in the profile exists to explain the score and guide deployment — it does not quietly add points to it. Move the three inputs below to see how the weighting behaves.

Founder Potential 60% 82/100

Founder Experience 20% 72/100

Creation and ownership, outcomes, complexity, attribution, durability.

Founder–Venture Fit 20% 85/100

Problem proximity, relevant knowledge, distinctive insight, access, capability.

60% × 82 + 20% × 72 + 20% × 85 = 81

Personality does not appear in this calculation. It explains behaviour and operating preference; it contributes no points.

This widget is an explanation of the weighting, not an assessment. Real component scores come from evidence, structured items, an interview and an expert assessor — never from a slider.

The six dimensions

What gets assessed, in full.

Three dimensions feed the score. Three explain it. All six appear on the profile, because a decision-maker needs the explanation as much as the number.

01 · Scored — 60%

Founder Potential

Eight mechanisms for building under uncertainty. Assessed as behaviour with evidence behind it, not as self-description.

  • Reality Contact — how directly beliefs meet the world. The single most common watch area.
  • Calibrated Agency — acting decisively while reading your own control accurately.
  • Opportunity Insight — identifying value others have looked at and missed.
  • Venture Learning Velocity — the speed at which belief actually changes on evidence.
  • Decision Quality Under Uncertainty — judged on the reasoning, not the outcome.
  • Disciplined Execution — the distance between what was intended and what shipped.
  • Resource Orchestration — assembling capital, people, access and attention.
  • Adaptive Resilience — recovering capability after a setback, not merely morale.

02 · Explanatory — 0%

Style and Operating Signature

How the founder naturally prefers to operate. Reported in full, weighted at nothing. Preference describes style, not ability.

  • Founder archetype — one of eight styles, with a three-letter marker.
  • Horizon — pulled by what could exist, or by what is in front of them.
  • Arena — creates value outward, through people and markets, or inward, through the system.
  • Mode — moves in order to learn, or resolves in order to move.
  • Personality signature — a plain-language read across five dimensions.
  • Team posture — how the founder behaves beside co-founders and reports.

03 · Explanatory

Founder Edges

Where this founder creates disproportionate value. Edges are what a venture is really buying, and what a complementary team has to cover when they are absent.

  • Commercial
  • Product
  • Technical
  • Operational
  • Financial
  • People
  • Strategic
  • Capital and governance

04 · Explanatory

Leadership and Role Readiness

Eight leadership behaviours, converted into role-fit hypotheses. Readiness is assessed for a seat held now, under this venture's conditions.

  • Direction setting  ·  Decision ownership  ·  Talent judgement  ·  Delegation
  • Accountability  ·  Difficult conversations  ·  Emotional regulation  ·  Stakeholder trust and leadership

Role hypotheses

  • CEO
  • COO
  • CPO
  • CTO
  • CCO / CRO
  • CFO
  • Venture Builder

05 · Scored — 20%

Venture and Stage Fit

Fit is contextual, and it expires. The same founder can be an outstanding bet at Validate and the wrong one at Scale.

  • Founder–market fit — proximity to the problem and the customer.
  • Founder edge — the specific advantage this venture can use.
  • Best-fit stage — where this founder is strongest today.
  • Current capacity — time, attention and obligation, honestly stated.
  • Commitment and readiness — what the founder is actually prepared to do.
  • Experience evidence — what has been proven, and where.

Venture stages

  1. Discover
  2. Validate
  3. Build
  4. Sell
  5. Scale
  6. Steward

06 · Explanatory

Team, Character and Risk

The dimension most often skipped, and most often decisive after the fact.

  • Character and integrity — assessed against evidence and conduct, not impression.
  • Team Quotient — how the founder raises or lowers the capability of the people around them.
  • Co-founder needs — a specification, not a personality preference.
  • Best team configuration — the shape of the team this founder needs.
  • Primary derailment risk — the one thing most likely to break this.
  • Development priorities — a short list, ordered.
  • Evidence confidence — how much weight the profile itself will bear.

Assessment inputs

A questionnaire alone would be worthless. So it is one input of many.

Self-report tells you how a founder sees themselves. Evidence tells you what they have done. An interview tells you what happens when the two are held against each other. FQ uses all three, and records which one each conclusion rests on.

Founder materials

  • Founder CV.
  • Pitch deck.
  • Relevant founder and venture information.
  • Evidence of previous experience, achievements and outcomes.

Structured assessment

  • Structured personality questionnaire.
  • Structured questions and answers.
  • Open-ended questions.
  • Situational questions.
  • Agree / disagree items.

Interview and verification

  • Founder interview.
  • Expert assessor observations.
  • Follow-up questions and evidence checks.
  • Assessment of the founder's response to challenge.
  • Clarification of achievements, experience and personal contribution.

Response to challenge

One input deserves singling out. A great deal of what separates founders shows up in the ninety seconds after a claim is questioned — whether the answer gets more specific or less. Founder Code assesses that deliberately, and reports it.
