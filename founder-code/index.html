01 — The gap

Two intelligences are measured. The one that builds ventures is not.

IQ and EQ are well instrumented and widely used. Neither describes how a founder operates under uncertainty, where they create disproportionate value, which role they should hold, which venture they fit, or who they need as a co-founder.

IQ

Cognitive intelligence

How quickly and how well a person reasons. Useful, well validated, and silent on everything a venture actually demands.

EQ

Emotional intelligence

How a person reads and regulates emotion — their own and other people's. Necessary for leadership. Not sufficient for founding.

FQ

Founder Quotient

How a founder thinks, operates, leads, builds and creates value — and how that fits a role, a venture, a stage and a founding team.

03 — The assessment

Six dimensions. One founder, seen from six directions.

A founder is not a single number. FQ looks through six lenses, and reports each one separately so a reader can see what is strong, what is thin and what still needs verifying.

The largest single contributor to the FQ score. Potential is treated as a set of observable mechanisms rather than a personality trait — things a founder does when the information is incomplete and the decision cannot wait.

  • Reality Contact — how directly beliefs are tested against the world.
  • Calibrated Agency — acting decisively without over-claiming control.
  • Opportunity Insight — seeing value where others see noise.
  • Venture Learning Velocity — how fast belief updates on evidence.
  • Decision Quality Under Uncertainty — the quality of the call, not the outcome.
  • Disciplined Execution — converting intention into shipped reality.
  • Resource Orchestration — assembling money, people and access.
  • Adaptive Resilience — recovering capability, not just morale.

Style explains behaviour. It is reported because it predicts how a founder will act inside a team — and it contributes no points to the FQ score. Preference describes style, not ability.

  • Founder archetype — one of the eight founder styles.
  • Horizon — vision or ground.
  • Arena — outward or inward.
  • Mode — momentum or rigour.
  • Personality signature — a plain-language read, not a diagnosis.
  • Team posture — how the founder shows up beside other people.

The dimension most often skipped and most often decisive. It covers character and integrity, Team Quotient, co-founder needs, best team configuration, the primary derailment risk, development priorities, and how much confidence the available evidence actually supports.

Evidence confidence is reported explicitly. A high score on thin evidence is a different decision from the same score on deep evidence, and the profile says which one you are looking at.

04 — The FQ score

Three components make the score. Everything else explains it.

Deliberately narrow. Most of what the profile reports — style, personality, team posture, derailment risk — is there to explain behaviour and guide deployment, not to inflate a number.

Try the weighting for yourself →

Founder Potential60% weight
Founder Experience20% weight
Founder–Venture Fit20% weight

60% × Potential  +  20% × Experience  +  20% × Venture Fit  =  FQ

Personality explains behaviour and operating preference. It adds no points.

05 — Operating signature

Eight founder styles. None of them better than another.

Style is the intersection of three preference axes — Horizon, Arena and Mode. Three binary axes give eight combinations, and each combination gets a three-letter marker that appears on the profile. Most founders are a blend.

07 — Who uses it

The same intelligence, read for a different decision.

Understand yourself before someone else has to.

Founders are rarely short of feedback and frequently short of structure. FQ gives you a blueprint: what you actually do well under uncertainty, which seat fits you now, which stage suits you, what is likely to derail you, and the specific kind of co-founder who would make you materially more effective.

Start with your style →

  • A clear view of your founder potential, edge for edge.
  • An honest read on whether CEO is the right seat for you today.
  • A co-founder specification you can actually recruit against.
  • A development roadmap with two or three priorities, not twenty.
  • Language for describing yourself to investors that survives scrutiny.

Diligence the founder as rigorously as the market.

Most funds have a repeatable process for the business and an improvised one for the person running it. FQ gives the investment committee a structured, comparable read on founder potential, experience evidence and founder–venture fit — plus an explicit list of what still needs verifying.

  • Comparable founder intelligence across a portfolio and a pipeline.
  • Evidence confidence stated on every profile, so thin data is visible.
  • A targeted diligence list rather than a general impression.
  • Team-risk and derailment hypotheses before the cheque clears.
  • Post-investment: role allocation and complementary hiring.

Match the founder to the venture you already have.

Studios and venture builders have the opposite problem to investors: the venture exists and the founder is missing. Founder Hunter searches, assesses and matches against the specific venture, stage and team you are staffing — and names the complementary support the placement will need.

How Founder Hunter works →

  • Founder and co-founder search against a defined venture thesis.
  • Stage-fit matching — Discover through Steward.
  • Role allocation across a founding team, not just a hire.
  • A complementary team blueprint for each placement.
  • A growing regional map of assessed founder talent.

Select better cohorts, and develop them precisely.

Accelerators, incubators, innovation programmes and government programmes make selection decisions at volume and development decisions under time pressure. FQ supports both: a structured basis for admission, and a per-founder development roadmap that tells a programme where its hours will actually pay.

  • Structured, comparable selection across a large applicant pool.
  • Cohort-level view of strengths, gaps and team configurations.
  • Per-founder development priorities for mentoring hours.
  • Co-founder matching inside and across cohorts.
  • Evidence of programme effect on founder capability over time.

Methodological position

Founder Code is an evidence-informed assessment under validation. Its outputs are structured hypotheses for expert verification, decision-support intelligence and a framework for targeted diligence. They are not deterministic facts, a psychological diagnosis, a pass-or-fail judgement, a replacement for expert assessment, or a replacement for commercial, financial, legal or investment diligence.
