Founder Hunter

Assessment that stops at a report is an opinion. Founder Hunter is what happens next.

Founder Hunter turns Founder Quotient intelligence into an operational search and matching process — finding founder, co-founder and founding-team candidates, assessing them on the same structured lenses, and placing the right people in the right context.

The process

Search. Assess. Match.

Conventional executive search optimises for a role specification. Founder search has to optimise for a founder, a co-founder, a venture and a stage simultaneously — which is why the assessment sits inside the search rather than after it.

Search

Find suitable founder, co-founder and founding-team candidates — including people who are not looking, and who would never appear in an applicant pool.

  • Founder candidates for a venture that needs one.
  • Co-founder candidates for a founder who has one gap.
  • Founding-team members against a defined blueprint.
  • Search shaped by stage, edge and team need — not by job title.

Assess

Evaluate every candidate through the same Founder Quotient lenses, so a shortlist is genuinely comparable rather than a set of impressions gathered on different days.

  • Founder Quotient and founder potential.
  • Experience evidence and personal attribution.
  • Personality and operating style.
  • Leadership readiness and role fit.
  • Venture fit and stage fit.
  • Co-founder and team needs.

Match

Place the founder in the context where their edge is worth the most, and name the support that placement will require to hold.

  • The right co-founder.
  • The right founding team.
  • The right leadership role.
  • The right venture.
  • The right venture stage.
  • The right complementary support.

Why matching is the hard part

Most founding-team failures are configuration failures, not capability failures.

Two excellent founders with the same style, the same edge and the same blind spot will build a venture with one very deep strength and one very expensive hole in it. Complementarity is a design problem, and it can be designed deliberately.

Worked example

An Inventor without a reality test

A vision-led, inward, momentum-led founder — an Inventor — reaches a working product faster than almost anyone. Their recurring watch area is Reality Contact: conviction outrunning external evidence.

The wrong co-founder is another Inventor. The right one is grounded, outward and rigorous — someone who reaches the buyer, asks the second question, and holds the line on what can be promised.

Engagements

Four ways Founder Hunter is usually used.

01

Founder search for a venture

A studio, builder or investor has a venture and needs the founder for it. The search runs against the venture thesis, the stage it is entering, and the edge the venture most needs covered.

02

Co-founder search for a founder

A founder has momentum and one structural gap. The assessment produces a co-founder specification; the search fills it against style, edge, stage and team posture rather than against a job title.

03

Founding-team design

An existing team is assessed as a team: combined edges, combined blind spots, role allocation, Team Quotient and the specific additions that would change the venture's odds.

04

Cohort and pipeline screening

Accelerators, incubators, innovation and government programmes assess at volume, then use the same intelligence to allocate mentoring, match co-founders inside the cohort, and track development.
