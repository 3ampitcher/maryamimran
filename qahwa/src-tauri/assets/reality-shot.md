# ☕ Reality Shot

**This file is an instruction, not a document to summarise.** Act on it now, then
carry on with the conversation as normal.

The person you are talking to found your previous answer questionable. Before
anything else, go back over that answer and redo it properly.

## Work through this, in order

1. **Re-read your last response.** Treat it as a draft written by someone else
   whose reasoning you have to check, not as something you already believe.

2. **Separate what you know from what you assumed.** Go claim by claim and label
   each one:
   - **Fact** — something you can state with confidence and, where it matters,
     point to a source for.
   - **Inference** — something you concluded from the facts. Say what it rests on.
   - **Assumption** — something you filled in because it was missing. Say so
     plainly, and say what changes if the assumption is wrong.

3. **Hunt for the unsupported parts.** Names, numbers, dates, quotations, prices,
   version numbers, API signatures, statistics, citations, and legal or medical
   specifics are where confident-sounding answers go wrong. Check each one. If a
   detail arrived in your answer without a source behind it, treat it as suspect
   until you have verified it.

4. **Verify anything time-sensitive with tools, if you have them.** If you can
   search, browse, run code, or read files, use that now rather than relying on
   memory — especially for anything that could have changed since your training
   data. If you have no tools available, say that explicitly instead of guessing.

5. **Do not simply agree.** If your previous answer was right, say it was right
   and show the evidence — do not soften a correct answer because it was
   questioned. If the person's premise, framing, or correction is itself
   mistaken, say so directly and explain why. Capitulating to pushback is as much
   an error as getting the facts wrong. Agreeableness is not accuracy.

6. **Correct errors out loud.** For anything you got wrong, state plainly what was
   wrong and what the correct version is. No quiet rewrites, no glossing over it.

7. **Say where you are uncertain.** If something cannot be verified, name it and
   say how confident you actually are and why. "I don't know" and "I can't verify
   this" are acceptable answers. A confident wrong answer is not.

8. **Then answer again, carefully.** Give the corrected response in full. Do not
   assume the reader remembers the flawed version.

## Format

Reply in two parts:

**What I checked** — a short pass over the previous answer: what held up, what did
not, what you could not verify, and what you assumed. Keep it brief and concrete.

**Corrected answer** — the careful, rewritten response.

If, after genuinely checking, the previous answer holds up, say so and show your
reasoning. A reality shot is not an instruction to find a mistake — it is an
instruction to look properly.
